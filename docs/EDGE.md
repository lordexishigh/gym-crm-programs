## What we're building to stand out

### Frozen Member Portal Decoupling

Billing state and portal access are stored as independent flags, so a frozen member still receives rehab or off-season programs through their pause.

**How it works:** The member record carries two independent boolean flags: billing_active and portal_active. Freezing a membership flips billing_active to false and stops charges; portal_active remains true by default (configurable per gym). The member portal shows a 'membership paused — resuming [date]' banner but the member can still view assigned programs and log sessions. Class booking is gated on billing_active, not portal_active. Staff see frozen members visually distinguished on the roster. The entire implementation is a data model decision — two columns, one flag per dimension — that costs almost nothin
**Closes:** Frozen members losing portal access precisely when retention work matters most — acutely felt in Cyprus's July–August seasonal pause.
**Scores:** impact 4/5 · effort 2/5 · novelty 4/5 · defensibility 2/5 · fit 5/5
**Risk:** Gyms that want frozen members fully locked out need a clear override path; default-open behaviour surprises if not explained during onboarding.
**Done when:** A member with billing_active=false can log into the portal, view an assigned program, and log a session; attempting to book a class returns a blocked message; the trainer's roster shows a visual freeze indicator; billing does not resume until explicitly unfrozen by staff.

## Gaps in the field

- **Personal training session packs — a declining-balance model structurally distinct from recurring memberships — have no native tracking in any incumbent. A member buys 20 PT sessions; each session consumed should decrement the balance, surface the remaining count to both trainer and member, and alert when it hits zero.** — dealbreaker
  - Who: Any gym running personal training alongside group memberships, which is the majority. The trainer delivering the sessions and the owner selling the packs both feel it — the trainer loses track of what a client has used; the owner cannot report on PT revenue cleanly.
  - Evidence: Trainers universally maintain a parallel spreadsheet or notebook tally. Mindbody approximates it with 'pricing options / class packs' but the UX collapses for multi-trainer PT scenarios. The workaround is so normalised that accountants who serve gyms cite session-pack reconciliation as a standard monthly problem. No incumbent lists native PT pack tracking as a feature.
  - Still unserved because: Session packs require a debit ledger per member-trainer pair, not a subscription ledger per member — decrement on session completion (not booking), handle partial use when a pack expires, and support refund logic for unused sessions. This is unglamorous engineering with no viral marketing value and does not appear in a feature comparison table. Incumbents deprioritise it despite its revenue importance. It is a solvable problem, not a structural trap.
- **Trial and prospect members exist in a state no product models. After a free class or drop-in, the gym has a warm contact who is not yet a paying member. Giving them full member-portal access is premature; leaving them as a bare CRM contact means the trainer cannot assign a taster program, the owner cannot track conversion rate, and no structured follow-up exists inside the product.** — real pain
  - Who: Gyms with a drop-in or trial-day model — boutique studios, PT-focused gyms, CrossFit boxes with free intro classes. The owner and the sales-minded trainer feel it when a warm prospect goes cold with no logged interaction to look back on.
  - Evidence: Gyms export trial contacts to a separate spreadsheet or use Mailchimp and Notion in parallel for trial follow-up. Trial-to-member conversion rate is a metric no gym CRM surfaces, even though it is the most actionable sales number a small gym has. The metric's absence is itself the evidence.
  - Still unserved because: Modelling a 'trial' state requires a distinct access tier and a state machine with its own portal rules, complicating the tenant data model. Most gym CRM founders came from the operations side and modelled the post-conversion member experience, treating top-of-funnel as another tool's problem. Adding this later means retrofitting access control — structurally costly for incumbents, but buildable from day one in a new product.
- **When a membership is frozen (injury, holiday, surgery), every incumbent shuts off member portal access entirely. Trainers who want to assign a rehab program, send mobility homework, or maintain the relationship through the pause have no in-product mechanism. The member goes dark precisely when retaining them matters most.** — real pain
  - Who: Gyms with a personal training focus or rehabilitation clientele, and any gym in a market with strong seasonality — Cyprus in July–August sees members pause for summer travel. The trainer loses contact at the highest-churn moment.
  - Evidence: The universal workaround: trainers send PDF programs via WhatsApp during a member's freeze. Returning members frequently cite the WhatsApp relationship as the reason they came back — meaning the CRM had zero involvement in the retention event it should have driven.
  - Still unserved because: Frozen members generate no revenue, so incumbents have no commercial incentive to invest in their experience. More structurally, the feature requires decoupling billing state from portal access state — a non-trivial change in any system that tied these together from day one. It is a solvable design problem, not a hard technical one, but the economics never justified it for a billing-first product team.
- **Trainers building programs have no in-app exercise library — no standardised exercise names, no default sets-reps schemes for common lifts, no video cues or movement descriptions attached to an exercise entry. Every program is built from a blank text field. Trainers maintain their exercise banks in Google Docs or Notion and copy-paste into the CRM, destroying the eight-minute program-build target.** — real pain
  - Who: Any trainer building individualised programs at volume, especially when onboarding several new members in the same week. The friction compounds when a trainer leaves and their personal exercise naming conventions go with them, breaking the program records for their former members.
  - Evidence: The absence is consistent across Mindbody, Glofox, PushPress, and Zen Planner. Platforms that have solved this — Trainerize, TrueCoach — are not gym management platforms and lack billing, check-in, and scheduling. The market has bifurcated. Trainers' shared Google Docs with 'our exercise list' are the visible tell.
  - Still unserved because: Building a quality exercise library is content work, not engineering — it requires curated video, taxonomy decisions, and ongoing maintenance. Incumbents that started as scheduling and billing tools never hired for it and treat it as a cost centre. An MVP workaround is achievable: let trainers build their own saved exercise entries once and reuse them forever, before investing in a curated database. That makes it a genuine opportunity rather than a trap.
- **Assigning the same program to a cohort — an 8-week strength cycle for everyone in a Monday/Wednesday group class — requires a trainer to manually clone and individually assign the program to each member one at a time. There is no group-assign, template-broadcast, or cohort concept.** — real pain
  - Who: CrossFit and functional fitness coaches, boot-camp instructors, and any trainer running periodised group progressions rather than purely one-to-one PT. Felt acutely at the start of a new training block when the trainer must touch every member record individually.
  - Evidence: PushPress with SugarWOD handles WOD posting — a single daily workout for everyone — but not periodised multi-week program assignment to a named group of members. The gap between 'post today's WOD' and 'assign this 6-week block to these 14 members' is unfilled. Trainers share a PDF or Google Sheet in a WhatsApp group so members can see the block collectively.
  - Still unserved because: Group program assignment sits between two product metaphors — the class scheduler, which manages attendance, and the individual program tool, which manages one-to-one coaching. Incumbents built these as separate modules that never connected. The feature requires a 'cohort' entity that is neither a class nor an individual — a third data model no incumbent has introduced. Not technically hard; architecturally requires a deliberate choice incumbents have not made.
- **Off-card payments — cash, bank transfer, and in Cyprus specifically SEPA credit transfers — are handled outside every major gym CRM. Gyms that collect an annual membership via bank transfer must record this manually in a separate ledger, breaking the unified revenue view and destroying trust in the platform's reporting.** — real pain
  - Who: Gym owners in Southern European and MENA markets where cash and bank transfer are culturally normal for large or annual transactions. In Cyprus, a 12-month membership paid by annual bank transfer is common. Bookkeepers reconciling gym accounts feel it every month when the CRM revenue figure does not match bank statements.
  - Evidence: Every reviewed platform is Stripe-first with no manual payment workflow. Gym owners in Cypriot and Greek Facebook groups consistently ask how to 'log a cash payment' — answers are always 'mark them as paid manually' with no audit trail. Revenue dashboards in these gyms never match actual revenue, so owners stop using them. A parallel Xero or notebook is the standard workaround.
  - Still unserved because: Stripe-first platforms are architected for US and UK markets where card payment is near-universal. Adding manual payment logging creates reconciliation liability the CRM vendor does not want to own — a bug that loses a logged cash payment creates a dispute the vendor cannot win. Incumbents leave it to integrations. For a product launching in Cyprus, this is low engineering cost, high trust value, and none of the incumbents will retrofit it for a small market. Genuine opportunity.
- **At the moment of check-in, staff see that a member has arrived but not what that member is supposed to do today, which day of their assigned program they are on, or when they last logged a session. The check-in event and the training program exist in unconnected views with no bridge between them.** — annoyance
  - Who: Trainers working a busy floor during peak hours — 07:00–09:00 and 18:00–20:00 — who are expected to give a brief personal acknowledgement to each arriving member. Without a contextual prompt, the interaction is generic. The breakdown becomes visible above roughly 40 active members, where a trainer can no longer memorise everyone's program day from memory.
  - Evidence: No incumbent surfaces program context at check-in. The workaround is a clipboard with a printed weekly plan grid pinned to the front desk, updated manually each week. The clipboard is the tell: it exists because the software never connected these two events.
  - Still unserved because: Connecting a real-time check-in event to program assignment data requires joining two subsystems incumbents built independently — often by different teams at different times. The query is technically straightforward but requires a data integration decision that was never made. Lower severity than other gaps because trainers adapt, but it directly undermines the member-personalisation value proposition of any program feature.
- **Trainers have no view of their own coaching portfolio: active assigned members, program adherence rate (sessions logged versus sessions assigned), retention rate within their member cohort, or which exercises their clients consistently skip or never log. Owner analytics exist across all incumbents; trainer-level self-analytics do not exist in any of them.** — annoyance
  - Who: Trainers who are compensated partly on client retention or renewal, or who are building a professional reputation and want data to show prospective clients. Also felt by owners who want to compare trainer effectiveness without manually constructing a report.
  - Evidence: Trainers who care about their own metrics export member lists and build personal tracking spreadsheets or use Notion. The absence is consistent across all incumbents. The metric that matters most to a trainer's livelihood — are my clients staying and progressing — is invisible inside the tool they use every day.
  - Still unserved because: Incumbents sell to gym owners, not trainers. The trainer is a user, not the buyer, so features that serve the trainer without a direct owner purchasing signal are consistently deprioritised. Trainer analytics also require workout-logging data as a prerequisite — which most incumbents lack, making this a second-order gap that only becomes available once the first-order program feature is built.

## Also considered (not building now)

- **Session-Missed Adherence Alert Queue** — The system tracks expected training frequency from each assigned program and surfaces an actionable queue when a member falls behind, with a pre-drafted message the trainer sends in one click. _(impact 4/5, effort 2/5)_
- **PT Session Pack Ledger** — A declining-balance account per member-trainer pair for personal training session bundles, fully integrated into the member profile and billing dashboard. _(impact 4/5, effort 3/5)_
- **Cohort Program Broadcast with Dry-Run Preview** — A trainer selects a program template and assigns it to multiple members at once; a dry-run preview shows each recipient's current state and flags conflicts before any records are written. _(impact 3/5, effort 3/5)_
- **Membership Expiry Renewal Queue with Pre-Built Payment Link** — Seven days before expiry, the system queues a draft renewal outreach pre-filled with the member's plan details and a live Stripe payment link, so the owner sends a personalised renewal in one click. _(impact 3/5, effort 2/5)_
- **Check-in Context Card** — When a member checks in, a non-blocking card appears on the staff screen showing that member's current program day, last logged session, and a pinned trainer note. _(impact 2/5, effort 2/5)_
- **Trial Member State Machine** — A first-class 'trial' member state with its own limited portal tier, so a drop-in guest can receive a taster program and be tracked through conversion without ever being a full paying member. _(impact 3/5, effort 3/5)_
